The 30-second version

A screen-time blocker where the unlock key is a chess puzzle.

MateToScroll locks the apps you pick once your daily limit runs out. Solving chess puzzles fills a minutes bank you spend to unlock them again, with 30,000 puzzles, 33 opening courses and calculation drills, all offline.

  • 168 msto lock an app

The problem

Blockers take time away. They don't give anything back.

Blockers like Opal stop you opening an app, but the time saved goes nowhere useful. Here you earn screen time by training chess. It's the mobile version of my desktop trainer, Chess Terminal.

  • No account, no server: nothing leaves the phone.
  • Never charge time it didn't actually see.
  • Pass Google Play's review of Accessibility apps.

What I built

A blocker on one side, a chess trainer on the other.

Lock screen

When a blocked app hits the limit, the lock screen opens and you can spend 5, 10 or 15 minutes from the bank.

The minutes bank

+1:00 per solved puzzle, +5:00 per boss, −0:30 per miss, with a daily cap and 2 emergency unlocks.

Animated board

Tap or drag pieces, with sound and haptics; puzzles are picked near your rating.

Level map

10 worlds from 600 to 2600 rating, each with 12 levels and a boss.

Openings and drills

33 opening courses against a book bot, plus four calculation drills including blindfold.

Onboarding

A short quiz sets your plan and starting rating, and a privacy notice comes before any permission.

The hard parts

Android doesn't make blocking apps easy.

01

Fair time-keeping

The service can be killed or the screen turned off. The engine charges at most 30 s per step and nothing across a restart, so you never lose minutes you didn't use.

02

Passing Play review

The service reads only the foreground app's name, never screen content, and the app list avoids the "all apps" permission.

03

307 MB of puzzles into 8.1 MB

A script samples 1,500 puzzles per rating band from the Lichess dump and checks each one with the app's own chess code.

How I worked

Plan in slices, check every one on a phone.

  • 21 slices, each with tests, a commit and a check on a real phone.
  • 188 Jest and 33 Kotlin tests, with a 90% coverage gate on the chess core.
  • No network calls at all; no CI yet.
  • Built with Claude Code as a coding assistant. I wrote the specs and made the product calls.

What I learned and what's next

What I learned

  • Timers that must survive the app dying belong in native code.
  • Android's rules shape the product as much as the design does.
  • Small slices tested on a real device kept 5 days under control.

What's next

  • Test the full lock-and-unlock loop on device.
  • Start a Google Play closed test.
  • Add a chess engine for more drills.
