"Which two companies share a supplier?" No single filing says.

The 30-second version

Answers across filings, with sources.

Ask questions about the 10-K reports of Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Microsoft and Tesla, and every answer cites its source. Vector search plus a knowledge graph lets it answer cross-company questions like "which two companies share a supplier?".

  • 78%correct on 151 questions
  • 12/12unanswerables refused
  • 443filing chunks
  • 616test cases

The problem

Search finds paragraphs. It doesn't connect them.

A 10-K is over a hundred pages. Finding one fact is easy; finding which of five companies name the same supplier means reading all five. Plain semantic search can miss a company that says it in other words.

  • Analysts looking for shared supply-chain risks.
  • Investors separating company risks from industry ones.
  • Researchers tracing who names whom as a competitor.

What I built

Ask, watch the agents work, check the sources.

Vector search + knowledge graph

The hard parts

Where the LLM needed guard rails.

01

Mixing two kinds of results

Vector scores and graph matches can't be ranked together. The graph gets a fixed half of the results so it isn't crowded out.

02

Noisy extraction

Free-form extraction made a new node for every phrasing. A fixed risk list and strict validation made companies connect.

03

Safe graph queries

The LLM only picks one of four hand-written queries instead of writing its own, so user input can't inject anything.

How I worked

Rules first, then one slice at a time.

  • Wrote the architecture rules and design decisions before coding.
  • Built in slices, from the service skeleton to hybrid retrieval and agents.
  • Scored with an LLM judge; human labels not added yet. Not deployed, and CI hasn't run yet.

What I learned and what's next

What I learned

  • A fixed vocabulary mattered more than the prompt.
  • When the answer space is small, plain code beats another LLM call.
  • Report by question type: multi-hop scored 66%, not 78%.

What's next
